# Psychological hooks CampusLoop marketing should lean on

A shared vocabulary for the ideas in this folder, so every reel/landing-page draft is reaching
for the same toolbox instead of reinventing persuasion from scratch each time.

1. **Future-self flirting** — talk to the person they're about to become, not the one they are
   today. "The senior who still has every note you're about to upload." "You, in four years,
   searching your own name and finding the group you built." Nostalgia for a memory that hasn't
   happened yet is stickier than any feature list.
2. **Belonging / in-group signal** — "verified students only," "your batch is already here,"
   "1,400+ colleges, yours included." People join rooms, not apps.
3. **Social proof** — live counters, "X people from your college joined this week," leaderboards,
   "top contributor" badges. Humans copy behavior they can see.
4. **FOMO / already-in-motion** — frame CampusLoop as a thing already happening without them,
   not a thing waiting to be built by them. "Your hostel wing's confessions page is already live."
5. **Curiosity gap** — withhold the punchline. "Someone from your branch just posted something
   about your professor." Open loops outperform complete sentences.
6. **Reciprocity** — give before asking. Free notes/PYQs/courses up front, no login wall, so the
   ask ("verify your college email") feels small by comparison.
7. **Identity rehearsal** — let the copy describe an identity ("the one who always has the notes,"
   "campus main character") the viewer wants to try on, then hand them the app as the costume.
8. **Low-stakes reversible entry** — "Preview mode," "guest access," "see it before you verify" —
   remove the fear of commitment that usually tanks sign-up rate on campus apps.

Rule for every asset below: **never lead with a single feature name.** Lead with a feeling or a
social dynamic; let the feature (courses, notes, confessions, match, communities) show up as the
proof, not the pitch.
